Butter Family Farms Rolls Out Multi-Factor Authentication and New Park Security Platform Ahead of Investor Audit

July 8, 2025 · Company News | Technology

Sandwich, Illinois — July 8, 2025

Butter Family Farms announced Tuesday a series of targeted security enhancements to its digital infrastructure, including the rollout of time-based one-time password (TOTP) authentication across priority systems and the deployment of a new internally developed Park Security Platform designed to centralize the park's security monitoring and incident response operations.

The enhancements are part of BFF's ongoing security investment ahead of the Bigger & Butter Project investor audit, which the company expects to begin in the fall.

Multi-Factor Authentication Rollout

Beginning July 1, TOTP-based multi-factor authentication has been enabled on a priority set of BFF's administrative systems. Robin McGrass, BFF's Head of Security, confirmed that MFA is now active on the park's employee management system, its financial reporting platform, the guest loyalty and ticketing back end, and executive email accounts.

"We identified the systems that carry the most sensitive data and started there," McGrass said. "It is not a full rollout across every platform yet. We are phasing it in deliberately so we do not break operational workflows while the park is running."

McGrass acknowledged that several systems, including some operational and point-of-sale platforms, remain on single-factor authentication while compatibility testing is completed. He said the team expects to expand MFA coverage to remaining administrative systems by end of Q3 2025.

IT Security Analyst Karen Ellis, who led the technical implementation, said the team evaluated several authenticator options before standardizing on TOTP for compatibility with existing staff devices.

"We wanted something that would work for everyone from the executive team to shift leads without requiring new hardware," Ellis said. "TOTP was the right call for where we are right now."

Staff have been issued setup instructions through the employee management system. Accounts that have not completed MFA enrollment on priority systems will be suspended from access beginning August 1.

Park Security Platform

Alongside the MFA rollout, BFF has deployed a new internally developed security tool the team is calling the Park Security Platform, or PSP. Built on a containerized architecture using Docker and written in Golang, the PSP aggregates security event data from across the park's network, interfaces with the park's existing Nessus vulnerability scanner, and provides the security team with a unified dashboard for monitoring, alerting, and incident tracking.

The platform has both internal and external facing components. The internal interface is accessible to McGrass, Ellis, and Network Administrator Jasmine Blunt from within the park's production network. An external interface allows McGrass to review high priority alerts and approve remediation tickets remotely.

"We were running everything manually before this," Ellis said. "Nessus would generate a report, I would go through it, flag things, and track remediation in a spreadsheet. The PSP gives us a real workflow. Everything is in one place."

The platform also integrates with the park's emergency notification system, enabling security alerts to be escalated to park operations leadership in real time during incidents.

McGrass noted that the PSP is an early-stage internal tool and that the team continues to develop and test its capabilities. He said the external-facing component in particular is still undergoing review before being considered fully production ready.

"We built it ourselves because nothing off the shelf fit exactly what we needed for a park environment," McGrass said. "That means we own it completely, which is good. It also means we are still finding the edges of it, which is why we are being careful about what we expose externally."

Looking Ahead

The security enhancements are the latest in a series of infrastructure improvements BFF has made in preparation for the investor audit phase of the Bigger & Butter Project. The company completed an external penetration test earlier this year, the findings of which are currently in active remediation.

Bigheart said the combined effect of the pen test findings, the MFA rollout, and the PSP deployment puts BFF in a significantly stronger position heading into investor conversations.

"Security used to be something we had to defend in a room," he said. "Now it is something we can demonstrate."
